Claude’s 25 concepts for non-programmers

Claude’s map in 5 parts: what each feature is, an example from everyday life, and which plan includes it.

See the 25 concepts Just 3 things to get started
In short

This guide explains, in simple language, the 25 Claude features (Anthropic’s AI assistant) that have appeared most often in the wave of news over the past few weeks. It is for people who use Claude at work but do not program: accountants, clinic owners, store owners, teachers. Each concept has a short explanation, an everyday example, and the plan that includes it. At the end, you’ll find a plans table, three steps to get started, and prompts ready to copy. Nothing to install: just read.

What it is

A map, not a manual

Claude has gained many new features in a short time. Instead of chasing every announcement, understand the 25 pieces and how they fit together.

🧠 No jargon

Each concept in 1 or 2 sentences. When a technical term appears, it is explained.

🧾 A real example

Accountant, clinic, store, teacher: one concrete case in each card, so you can picture how it is used.

🏷️ The plan for each feature

Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, or Team: you’ll know what you already have and what requires an upgrade, checked against the official pages in November 2026.

Part 1 · concepts 1 to 5

The brain: which model, how much it can handle, how much it thinks

Before any feature, understand the engine: the models, the unit of measure (tokens), effort, and your plan’s limits.

1

Models: Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus 5.5

Claude comes in different sizes. Haiku is fast and inexpensive, Sonnet is for everyday work, and Opus is for big, messy jobs.

Everyday example: an accountant uses Sonnet to answer client questions and switches to Opus when they need to review the entire month’s closing.

Plan Free: Sonnet and Haiku. Opus starting with Pro.

2

Fable 5.1, the top model

The strongest model, for demanding reasoning and long jobs. It costs 2.5 times as much as Opus per use, and Anthropic itself says Opus 5.5 reaches Fable’s level for most work.

Everyday example: a teacher saves Fable for putting together a plan for an entire semester; Opus handles the rest of the week.

Plan Pro: only by paying for usage credits. Max and Ultra: up to half the weekly limit. Team: only with a Premium seat.

3

Tokens and context window

A token is a piece of a word; it is used to measure cost and limits. The context window is how much Claude can hold at once: in current models, up to 1 million tokens, or hundreds of thousands of words. Tip: what you load (files, standing instructions) weighs more than your question.

Everyday example: a clinic attaches its entire procedure manual and asks only what changed in sterilization. The manual takes up almost the whole allowance; the question, almost none.

Plan All. Up to 1 million tokens, depending on the model.

4

Effort level

How much Claude thinks before answering: low, medium, high, xhigh, and max. In the 5.5 models, the default is medium; for Fable, it is high. According to the documentation, max tends to overthink: test it before using it regularly. In Claude Code, change it with /effort.

Everyday example: a store owner keeps effort low for answering customers and raises it to high when asking for an analysis of quarterly sales.

Plan Adjust with /effort in Claude Code (Pro and above).

5

Usage limits and plans

Almost every plan has two clocks: one for 5 hours and one for the week. They apply together and are shared across the web, computer, phone, and Claude Code. On Ultra, a verified account only has the weekly limit. Check yours in Settings > Usage (or /usage in Claude Code).

Everyday example: a teacher who grades papers on their phone in the morning and prepares lessons on the computer in the afternoon uses the same allowance.

Plan See the plans table.

Part 2 · concepts 6 to 11

Where to use it: chat, Code, Cowork, and the new Duet

The same Claude shows up in different places. Knowing which door to open saves time and usage limits.

6

Chat

The classic Claude: you ask, it answers. Since September, it has also created documents, presentations, and designs within the conversation itself.

Everyday example: a store owner asks, “put together 6 slides about the Christmas sale,” and gets the presentation right there.

Plan All, including Free.

7

Claude Code

The Code tab in the computer app. It works inside one of your folders: reads, creates, and edits files and runs commands. The name sounds intimidating, but it is not just for programmers.

Everyday example: an accountant points it to a folder with 300 PDF invoices and asks for a spreadsheet with supplier, date, and amount.

Plan Pro and above. Not available on Free.

8

Cowork

The mode for handing over an entire office job and coming back to review it. Since 16/09/2026, it has been alongside chat in the same Claude and keeps working even after you close your laptop.

Everyday example: an office asks it to review a folder of 40 rental agreements against its checklist and flag anything that falls outside the rules.

Plan First arrived on Pro, Max, and Ultra; later Team and Free.

9

Sessions and split view

Each conversation in the Code tab is a session with its own history. Open several (Ctrl+N or Cmd+N) and place two side by side with Alt+click (Option on Mac). Remote Control connects the session to the phone app so you can follow along and continue from there.

Everyday example: the monthly report in one session, customer replies in another, both on the same screen.

Plan Included with Claude Code (Pro and above).

10

Side chats

A parallel conversation for asking a question without interrupting the task. Open it with Ctrl+; (Cmd+; on Mac) or /btw. It can see the main conversation but does not change it. The computer app does not save side chats.

Everyday example: in the middle of a large spreadsheet, an accountant asks, “what does tax code 5.102 mean again?” without interrupting the work.

Plan Included with Claude Code (Pro and above).

11

Claude Duet

The new agent you interact with like a person, by text or voice. Every morning it checks email and your calendar, flags what needs attention, drafts replies, and follows up on stalled conversations. It asks before sending, paying, signing, or deleting. It runs on its own computer in the cloud.

Everyday example: it reminds a store owner about a supplier who has not replied in a week and drafts a follow-up for approval.

Plan In testing: waitlist, US only, Pro, Max, and Ultra. Not available on Team yet.

Part 3 · concepts 12 to 15

What it does: documents, dashboards, and animation

Claude delivers more than text. It delivers the finished thing, alongside the conversation.

12

Artifacts

What Claude creates and opens beside the chat: a document, presentation, design, dashboard, or small tool. It starts out private; you decide whether to share it.

Everyday example: a teacher asks for a class average calculator and sends the link to the school office.

Plan All, including Free.

13

Docs, Slides, and Design

Documents, presentations, and visual pieces made by Claude. They left beta on 08/10/2026 and are available on all plans. The separate site claude.ai/design stays online until 14/12/2026; after that, it will be read-only.

Everyday example: a clinic creates a post-visit care brochure and a presentation for its new team.

Plan All, including Free.

14

Dashboards

Ask in plain English and Claude builds a dashboard from your data and keeps it updated. Connect it to databases such as BigQuery, Snowflake, Databricks, and Redshift. Click a number to see the query behind it; each chart shows when it was updated.

Everyday example: a manager at a chain of stores with sales in one of these databases sees the weekly results for each store without building a spreadsheet.

Plan Beta, paid plans only.

15

Motion

Turns an idea into an animated explainer that plays like a video and downloads as an MP4. It is made with code, without a video generator or AI-generated people.

Everyday example: a school makes a 30-second animation explaining the enrollment process step by step.

Plan Beta, Team and Enterprise only.

Part 4 · concepts 16 to 21

What it knows about you: memory, instructions, and extensions

Every conversation starts from scratch. These six pieces carry forward who you are, how you work, and which tools you use.

16

Memory

Memory carries important information into future conversations. Claude Code also has automatic memory: notes Claude writes based on your corrections and preferences.

Everyday example: Claude remembers that an accountant serves only MEI and Simples Nacional businesses and does not ask again.

Plan All, including Free.

17

CLAUDE.md

An instruction file you write that Claude reads at the start of every session. Memory is what Claude writes down; CLAUDE.md is what you write down. Keep it short: everything there is loaded every time.

Everyday example: “Use simple English. Our clients are small clinics. Never send anything without my approval.” See a ready-to-use template.

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

18

Skills

A file (SKILL.md) with the right way to do a task. Claude uses it when it is relevant, or you call it with /name. Until it is used, only its name and description take up space.

Everyday example: a store creates the /quote skill, which always prepares a quote using the company’s template, with a deadline and payment terms.

Plan All, including Free.

19

Connectors

They connect Claude to the apps you already use, so it can read and work with them. Many use MCP (Model Context Protocol), an open standard for this connection.

Everyday example: with its calendar connected, a clinic asks, “what times are free on Thursday?” and Claude checks the actual calendar to answer.

Plan All, including Free.

20

Plugins

A package that installs Skills, agents, automatic rules (hooks), and MCP connectors all at once. There is an official marketplace for finding ready-made plugins.

Everyday example: an office installs a ready-made package for sales instead of setting up each piece one by one.

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

21

Mods

A type of plugin that changes how Claude Code looks and behaves: panels beside the conversation, a strip above the text field, new commands. You do not need to program: describe what you want and Claude writes it. Mods do not run in isolation, so check before installing one from someone else.

Everyday example: “make a mod that shows above the prompt how many tasks are left today.”

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

Part 5 · concepts 22 to 25 + bonus

Working on its own: permission, helpers, and the finish line

Here Claude stops being a conversation and becomes a colleague who gets things done. You decide how much it can do without asking.

22

Permission modes

How much Claude can do without asking permission: Manual, Accept Edits, Plan, Auto, and Bypass. Shift+Tab switches between them. In Auto, a checker reviews each action before it runs; in the current version, it is already the default on all plans. Worried? Start in Plan mode.

Everyday example: in Plan mode, Claude shows an accountant what it will do with the 300 invoices before touching any file.

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

23

Subagents

Helpers with their own working memory. They handle one part of a task and return only a summary, keeping the main conversation clear. They use the same limit as you.

Everyday example: a subagent reads 50 résumés and returns the 5 closest matches for the job, with a reason for each.

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

24

Projects

In the app, a Project is a folder of conversations with files and standing instructions. In Claude Code, Projects means something else: an ongoing conversation that opens a work thread for each task, usually in the cloud, and continues while your computer is off. It uses up your limit faster.

Everyday example: the “Clinic” Project stores the price list and message style; every conversation there starts with that information.

Plan In the app: Free allows up to 5, unlimited starting with Pro. In Claude Code: beta on Pro, Max, and Ultra, rolling out gradually.

25

/goal

Give Claude a clear, verifiable finish line, and it works until it crosses it. After each step, a model checks whether the condition has been met; if not, Claude continues on its own. It stops when the condition is met, when it judges the task impossible, or when an error needs you to fix it.

Everyday example: “/goal all 300 invoices are in the spreadsheet and the sum matches the monthly statement.” More examples.

Plan Claude Code (Pro and above).

+

Bonus: scheduled tasks

Tasks that run on their own at a set time. In the computer app, they run on your machine. In the cloud (the former “routines,” now “scheduled tasks”), they can start on a schedule, a GitHub event, or a call; start with /schedule.

Everyday example: every Monday at 8 a.m., the previous week’s sales summary is ready for the meeting.

Plan Pro and above. In the cloud, still in testing (Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise).

Where to start

Just 3 things

You do not need to use all 25 tomorrow. These three can already change your day.

1

A short CLAUDE.md

Ten lines about you, your work, and the rules Claude must never break. Keep it short, because it is read in every session. Use the ready-to-use template.

2

A skill for the request you repeat most often

That request you type every week (a quote, a reply to a patient, a report) becomes a /command. Use the skill prompt.

3

Your next big task with /goal

Choose a big task and write a finish line you can check: a count, a sum, a file that needs to exist. See the examples.

Ready-to-use prompts

Copy, replace what’s in brackets, and use

Three templates for the first three steps.

1. CLAUDE.md template for non-programmers

Save it as CLAUDE.md in the folder where you work with Claude Code.

# Who I am
I am a [profession] in [city]. I serve [type of client].

# How to respond
- Use simple English, short sentences, and no jargon.
- When information is missing, ask before making anything up.
- Amounts in your local currency, dates in mm/dd/yyyy.

# Rules that must never be broken
- Never send, delete, or pay for anything without my approval.
- Client data stays in this folder; do not copy it elsewhere.

# Where things are
- Document templates: [templates/ folder]
- Main spreadsheet: [file.xlsx]

2. Turn a repeated request into a skill

Paste this into Claude after you have done the task with it once, in the same conversation.

We just did [the task, e.g., the client’s quote].
I do this every week. Turn what we did into a skill
called /[short-name]:
- write the SKILL.md with a name, a description of when to use it, and the steps;
- include the exact output format I approved here;
- list what you need to ask me when information is missing;
- do not make up rules I did not give you.
Then show me the skill and wait for my approval before saving it.

3. Three /goal examples with a verifiable finish line

A good finish line can be checked without opinion: a count, a sum, a file that exists.

# Accountant
/goal the spreadsheet october-invoices.xlsx has one row for each PDF
in the invoices/ folder and the sum of the Amount column matches the statement total

# Clinic
/goal each of the 12 procedures on the list has a care brochure
in PDF format in the brochures/ folder, with no more than 1 page each

# Teacher
/goal all 8 classes for the term have a lesson plan, exercise, and answer key
in the term-4/ folder, and the index.md lists all 8 with links
