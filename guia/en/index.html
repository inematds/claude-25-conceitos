The 25 Claude concepts for people who don’t code

A map of Claude in 5 parts: what each feature is, an everyday example, and which plan it’s available on.

See the 25 Concepts Just 3 Things to Get Started
In Short

This guide explains, in plain English, the 25 Claude features (Anthropic’s AI assistant) that have been appearing most often in recent weeks’ announcements. It’s for people who use Claude at work but don’t code: accountants, clinic owners, store owners, teachers. Each concept has a short explanation, an everyday example, and the plan it’s available on. At the end, you’ll find a plans table, three steps to get started, and ready-to-copy prompts. You don’t need to install anything to read this; some features require the Pro plan and the desktop app.

What It Is

A Map, Not a Manual

Claude has gained a lot of new features in a short time. Instead of chasing every announcement, understand the 25 pieces and how they fit together.

🧠 No Jargon

Each concept in 1 or 2 sentences. When a technical term appears, it’s explained.

🧾 A Real Example

Accountant, clinic, store, teacher: one concrete case in each card, so you can picture how to use it.

🏷️ Plan for Each Feature

Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, or Team: you’ll know what you already have and what requires an upgrade, checked against official pages in November 2026.

Part 1 · concepts 1 to 5

The Brain: Which Model, How Much It Can Handle, How Much It Thinks

Before any feature, understand the engine: the models, the measurement (tokens), the effort level, and your plan’s limits.

1

Models: Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus 5.5

Claude comes in different sizes. Haiku is fast and inexpensive, Sonnet is for everyday use, and Opus handles big, messy jobs.

In everyday life: an accountant uses Sonnet to answer client questions and switches to Opus when they need to review the entire month’s closing.

Plan Free: Sonnet and Haiku. Opus from Pro onward.

2

Fable 5.1, the Top Model

The most powerful model, for demanding reasoning and long tasks. It costs 2.5 times as much as Opus per use, and Anthropic itself says Opus 5.5 reaches Fable’s level for most work.

In everyday life: a teacher saves Fable for planning an entire semester; for the rest of the week, Opus does the job.

Plan Pro: only by paying for extra usage separately. Max and Ultra: up to half the weekly limit. Team: only on the Premium seat.

3

Tokens and the Context Window

A token is a piece of a word; it’s how cost and limits are measured. The context window is how much Claude can hold at once: up to 1 million tokens in current models, or hundreds of thousands of words. Tip: what you load (files, fixed instructions) weighs more than your question.

In everyday life: a clinic attaches its entire procedures manual and asks only what changed in sterilization. The manual uses nearly all the allowance; the question, almost none.

Plan All plans. Up to 1 million tokens, depending on the model.

4

Effort Level

How much Claude thinks before responding: low, medium, high, extra high (xhigh), and maximum (max). For 5.5 models, the default is medium; for Fable, high. According to the documentation, maximum tends to overthink: test it before using it every time. In Claude Code, change it with /effort.

In everyday life: in Claude Code, a store owner keeps effort low to rename product photos and raises it to high when asking for a quarterly sales analysis.

Plan Adjust with /effort in Claude Code (Pro onward).

5

Usage Limits and Plans

Almost every plan has two clocks: a 5-hour one and a weekly one. They apply together and are shared across the web, desktop, mobile, and Claude Code. On Ultra, verified accounts only have the weekly limit. See yours in Settings > Usage (or /usage in Claude Code).

In everyday life: a teacher who grades papers on their phone in the morning and prepares lessons on a computer in the afternoon draws from the same balance.

Plan See the plans table.

Part 2 · concepts 6 to 11

Where to Use It: Chat, Code, Cowork, and the New Duet

The same Claude appears in different places. Knowing which door to open saves time and usage limits.

6

Chat

The classic Claude: you ask, it answers. Since September, it can also create documents, presentations, and designs right in the conversation.

In everyday life: a store owner asks, “put together 6 slides about the Christmas promotion,” and gets the presentation right there.

Plan All plans, including Free.

7

Claude Code

The Code tab in the desktop app. It works inside one of your folders: reads, creates, and edits files, and runs commands. The name sounds intimidating, but it isn’t just for programmers.

In everyday life: an accountant points it to a folder with 300 PDF invoices and asks for a spreadsheet with supplier, date, and amount.

Plan Pro onward. Not available on Free.

8

Cowork

A mode for handing off an entire office task and coming back to review it. Since 16/09/2026, it has been alongside chat in the same Claude and keeps working even after you close your laptop.

In everyday life: an office asks it to review a folder of 40 rental contracts against its checklist and flag anything that doesn’t follow the rules.

Plan Released first on Pro, Max, and Ultra; Team and Free follow gradually.

9

Sessions and Split View

Each conversation in the Code tab is a session with its own history. Open several (Ctrl+N or Cmd+N) and place two side by side with Alt+click (Option on Mac). Remote Control connects the session to the mobile app so you can follow along and continue there.

In everyday life: the monthly report in one session, client replies in another, both on the same screen.

Plan Included with Claude Code (Pro onward).

10

Side Chats

A parallel conversation for asking a question without interrupting the task. Open one with Ctrl+; (Cmd+; on Mac) or /btw. It can see the main conversation, but doesn’t change it. The desktop app doesn’t save side chats.

In everyday life: in the middle of a large spreadsheet, an accountant asks, “what does tax code 5.102 mean again?” without derailing the work.

Plan Included with Claude Code (Pro onward).

11

Claude Duet

The new agent you interact with like a person, by text or voice. Every morning, it checks email and calendar, points out what needs attention, drafts replies, and follows up on stalled conversations. It asks before sending, paying, signing, or deleting. It runs on its own computer in the cloud.

In everyday life: it reminds a store owner about the supplier who hasn’t replied in a week and drafts a follow-up for approval.

Plan In testing: waitlist, US only, Pro, Max, and Ultra. Not yet on Team.

Part 3 · concepts 12 to 15

What It Does: Documents, Dashboards, and Animation

Claude doesn’t just deliver text. It delivers the finished work beside the conversation.

12

Artifacts

Things Claude creates and opens beside the chat: a document, presentation, design, dashboard, or small tool. They start out private; you decide whether to share them.

In everyday life: a teacher asks for a class average calculator and sends the link to the school coordinator.

Plan All plans, including Free.

13

Docs, Slides, and Design

Documents, presentations, and visual pieces made by Claude. They left beta on 08/10/2026 and are available on all plans. The separate site claude.ai/design stays online until 14/12/2026; after that, read-only.

In everyday life: a clinic creates a post-visit care leaflet and a presentation for its new team.

Plan All plans, including Free.

14

Dashboards

Ask in plain English, and Claude builds a dashboard from your data and keeps it up to date. It connects to company databases such as BigQuery, Snowflake, Databricks, and Redshift. Click a number to see the query behind it; each chart shows when it was updated.

In everyday life: a manager at a chain of stores, with sales data in one of these databases, sees the week’s results for each store without building a spreadsheet.

Plan Beta, paid plans only.

15

Motion

Turns an idea into an animated explainer that plays like a video and downloads as an MP4. It’s made with code, without a video generator or AI-generated people.

In everyday life: a school makes a 30-second animation explaining the enrollment steps.

Plan Beta, Team and Enterprise only.

Part 4 · concepts 16 to 21

What It Knows About You: Memory, Instructions, and Extensions

Every conversation starts from scratch. These six pieces carry forward who you are, how you work, and which tools you use.

16

Memory

Memory carries important details into future conversations. Claude Code also has automatic memory: notes Claude writes based on your corrections and preferences.

In everyday life: Claude remembers that an accountant only serves sole proprietors and Simples Nacional tax-regime clients, and doesn’t ask again.

Plan All plans, including Free.

17

CLAUDE.md

An instruction file you write and Claude reads at the start of each session. Memory is what Claude writes down; CLAUDE.md is what you write down. Keep it short: everything in it is loaded every time.

In everyday life: “Reply in plain English. Our clients are small clinics. Never send anything without my approval.” See a ready-to-use template.

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

18

Skills

A file (SKILL.md) describing the right way to do a task. Claude uses it when relevant, or you can call it with /name. Until it’s used, only its name and description take up space.

In everyday life: a store creates the /quote skill, which always prepares quotes using the store’s template, with a deadline and payment method.

Plan All plans, including Free.

19

Connectors

They connect Claude to the apps you already use, so it can read and take action in them. Many use MCP (Model Context Protocol), an open standard for this connection.

In everyday life: with its calendar connected, a clinic asks, “what times do I have available on Thursday?” and Claude answers by checking the actual calendar.

Plan All plans, including Free.

20

Plugins

A package that installs Skills, agents, automatic rules (hooks), and MCP connectors all at once. There’s an official marketplace for finding ready-made plugins.

In everyday life: an office installs a ready-made package for sales instead of setting up each piece one by one.

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

21

Mods

A type of plugin that changes how Claude Code looks and behaves: panels beside the conversation, a bar above the text box, new commands. You don’t need to code: describe what you want and Claude writes it. Mods don’t run in isolation, so check before installing one from someone else.

In everyday life: “make a mod that shows how many tasks are left today above the prompt.”

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

Part 5 · concepts 22 to 25 + bonus

Working on Its Own: Permission, Helpers, and the Finish Line

Here Claude stops being a conversation and becomes a coworker who gets things done. You decide how much it can do without asking.

22

Permission Modes

How much Claude can do without asking permission: Manual, Accept Edits, Plan, Auto, and Bypass. Shift+Tab switches between them. In Auto, a checker reviews each action before it runs; in the current version of Claude Code, it’s already the starting mode. Unsure? Start in Plan mode.

In everyday life: in Plan mode, Claude shows an accountant what it will do with the 300 invoices before changing any files.

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

23

Subagents

Helpers with their own working memory. They handle one part of a task and return only a summary, keeping the main conversation clean. They use the same limit as you.

In everyday life: a subagent reads 50 resumes and returns the 5 best matches for the job, with a reason for each.

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

24

Projects

In the app, a Project is a folder of conversations with files and fixed instructions. In Claude Code, Projects means something else: an ongoing conversation that starts a line of work for each task, usually in the cloud, and keeps going with your computer off. This uses up your limit faster.

In everyday life: the “Clinic” Project stores the price list and message style; every conversation there starts with that information.

Plan In the app: Free allows up to 5; unlimited from Pro onward. In Claude Code: beta on Pro, Max, and Ultra, rolling out gradually.

25

/goal

Give Claude a clear, verifiable finish line, and it works until it crosses it. After each step, a model checks whether the condition has been met; if not, Claude continues on its own. It stops when the condition is met, it judges the task impossible, or an error needs you to step in.

In everyday life: “/goal all 300 invoices are in the spreadsheet and the sum matches the monthly statement total.” More examples.

Plan Claude Code (Pro onward).

+

Bonus: Scheduled Tasks

Tasks that run on their own at a set time. In the desktop app, they run on your machine. In the cloud (the old “routines,” now “scheduled tasks”), they can run on a schedule, in response to a GitHub event, or when called; start with /schedule.

In everyday life: every Monday at 8 a.m., a summary of the previous week’s sales is ready for the meeting.

Plan Pro onward. In the cloud, still in testing (Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise).

Where to Start

Just 3 Things

You don’t need to use all 25 tomorrow. These three will already change your day.

First: all three steps use Claude Code, which requires the Pro plan and the Claude desktop app (Code tab, with one of your folders open). When working with client files, switch to Plan mode first (Shift+Tab): Claude shows you what it will do before taking action. Only use the browser? Start with step 1 by setting up Project instructions: open a Project, paste the same text into the instructions, and every conversation there will start with that information.

1

A Short CLAUDE.md

Ten lines about you, your work, and the rules Claude must never break. Keep it short, because it’s read in every session. Use the ready-to-use template.

2

A Skill for the Request You Repeat Most

That request you type every week (a quote, a patient reply, a report) becomes a /command. Use the skill prompt.

3

Your Next Big Task with /goal

Choose a big task and write a finish line you can check: a count, a sum, a file that needs to exist. See the examples.

Ready-to-Use Prompts

Copy, Replace What’s in Brackets, and Use

Three templates for the first three steps.

1. CLAUDE.md Template for People Who Don’t Code

In the Code tab, with your folder open, ask: “create a CLAUDE.md file in this folder with the text below.” Replace the bracketed text first. In the browser, paste the same text into a Project’s instructions.

# Who I am
I’m a [profession] in [city]. I serve [type of client].

# How to respond
- Use plain English, short sentences, and no jargon.
- When information is missing, ask before making anything up.
- Use [your local currency] for amounts, dates in mm/dd/yyyy format.

# Rules that must never be broken
- Never send, delete, or pay for anything without my approval.
- Client data stays in this folder; don’t copy it elsewhere.

# Where things are
- Document templates: [templates/ folder]
- Main spreadsheet: [file.xlsx]

2. Turn a Repeated Request into a Skill

Paste this into the Code tab after completing a task with Claude once, in the same conversation. The skill becomes a file in your folder, and from then on you call it with /short-name.

We just completed [the task, e.g., the client’s quote].
I do this every week. Turn what we just did into a skill
called /[short-name]:
- write the SKILL.md with a name, a description of when to use it, and the steps;
- include the exact output format I approved here;
- list what you need to ask me when information is missing;
- don’t make up rules I didn’t give you.
Then show me the skill and wait for my approval before saving it.

3. Three /goal Examples with Verifiable Finish Lines

Type these in the Code tab’s message field, with the work folder open (the folders mentioned must exist inside it). A good finish line can be checked without opinion: a count, a sum, a file that exists.

# Accountant
/goal the spreadsheet october-invoices.xlsx has one row for every PDF
in the invoices/ folder, and the sum of the Amount column matches the total from the file statement-october.pdf

# Clinic
/goal each of the 12 procedures on the list has a care leaflet
in PDF format in the leaflets/ folder, each no more than 1 page

# Teacher
/goal all 8 lessons for the term have a lesson plan, exercise, and answer key
in the term-4/ folder, and the index.md file lists all 8
